Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Dearborn

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year if their water is hard.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or moisture under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and causes far less stress than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a flooded utility room.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Long before a tank floods a Dearborn garage, it usually sends signals. Pay attention to rumbling and popping while the tank heats, rust-colored hot water, a faint rotten-egg smell, a damp spot under the tank, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. These are early signs of sediment buildup, a spent anode rod, a failing element, or corrosion starting at the bottom seam, and when they're caught early, most of them mean a modest repair or a replacement on your schedule. Left alone, they end in a failed tank and water on the floor. A dripping T&P valve deserves special attention: it's the tank telling you pressure or temperature is running too high, and it should be checked promptly. We find the root cause instead of treating symptoms, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
