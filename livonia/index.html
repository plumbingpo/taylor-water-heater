Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Livonia

Many early failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually includes the month and year it was built, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that range and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts today so help is one call away the moment something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Whatever type of water heater your Livonia home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tanks usually give trouble through pilot assemblies and thermocouples, dirty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric units lose elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless units show error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition faults, and flow sensor problems, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. We service all four types regularly and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
