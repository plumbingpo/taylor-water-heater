How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Rusty or brown water from the hot side only
  • A tank at or past the eight-to-twelve-year mark
  • Visible corrosion around the pipe connections or burner area
  • A slow leak or damp spot under the tank itself
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • Energy bills rising while hot water gets harder to come by
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater
  • Less and less hot water, even after repairs

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

A water heater swap seems easy right up until you count the connections. A gas unit needs a leak-free gas connection with a sediment trap, a vent that is sized and pitched correctly so carbon monoxide goes outside, and combustion air it can actually reach. An electric unit needs a circuit and breaker matched to the element wattage. Every tank needs a correctly rated relief valve with a discharge pipe routed to a safe spot, and many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps to meet local code. Going tankless or switching to a heat pump adds even more: bigger gas lines, different vent materials, dedicated circuits, and condensate drains.

What you get from a pro is a replacement that is done once. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here's where to begin:

  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
  • Keep the space around a heat pump model clear so it can draw heat from the room
  • Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safety and slower mineral buildup
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
  • Have water pressure checked; above about 80 psi, a pressure reducing valve protects the new heater
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
