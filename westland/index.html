Keeping Your Westland Water Heater Running Longer

Many premature tank failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the month and year it was built, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that range and is also showing rusty water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, neglected through a few more years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner.
