If you could see inside your water heater, the case for maintenance would make itself. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, creates popping and rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, built to corrode in place of the tank, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. For homeowners in Taylor, MI, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

A Taylor Water Heater visit is designed to stop both of those processes early. We drain and flush the tank until the water runs clear, remove and check the anode rod and recommend a replacement when it is mostly consumed, and test the temperature and pressure relief valve to confirm it opens and reseats. On gas units we inspect the burner, pilot or igniter, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue baffle, and venting for proper draft. On electric units we check element resistance and thermostat operation and look at the wiring for heat damage. We check the expansion tank's air charge against your water pressure, verify the temperature setting is near 120 degrees to limit scalding, and look for early leaks at fittings and valves.

Tankless systems are included. In place of a sediment flush, they need periodic descaling, since hard water leaves mineral deposits in the heat exchanger that restrict flow and reduce efficiency. We isolate the unit, circulate a descaling solution through it, clean the inlet filter, check the vent and condensate line, and look at the error history for patterns. Whatever type you have, you receive a plain summary of the unit's condition and any parts approaching the end of their life. For water heater maintenance in Taylor, call (734) 280-6312.

Signs You Need Water Heater Maintenance in Taylor

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Rumbling, popping, or crackling from the tank as it heats
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water
  • Shorter showers before the water turns lukewarm
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • A water heater that has never been flushed or inspected
  • Higher gas or electric costs for the same routine
  • Scale warnings or intermittent shutdowns on a tankless system
  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Have the anode rod checked every few years and replaced when it is mostly gone
  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Keep at least a couple of feet clear around the heater for air and service access
  • Set the water temperature around 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Make an annual flush part of your home maintenance routine
  • Stay on top of tankless descaling to avoid error codes and reduced flow
