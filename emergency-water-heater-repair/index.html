How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • The smell of gas near the unit, a situation that calls for evacuating and phoning the utility or 911
  • Sudden loud knocking or boiling sounds coming from inside the tank
  • Tap water hot enough to burn, even at the usual setting
  • The relief valve on the side of the tank dripping, hissing, or discharging
  • Signs of scorching or backdrafting around the base or top of a gas heater
  • No hot water at all, even after waiting for the tank to recover
  • An electric water heater that keeps tripping its circuit breaker
  • Standing water around the water heater that returns after you wipe it up

Why Hire a Professional for Emergency Water Heater Repair?

A water heater emergency packs several hazards into one closet or garage corner. There is water on the floor near a 240-volt circuit on electric models, or near a gas valve, burner, and flue on gas units. There is a tank holding water at high temperature and household pressure. And there is a safety valve that, if it is discharging, has a reason for it. Homeowners sometimes try to stop the relief valve by capping it, crank on a leaking fitting until it snaps, or relight a pilot beside a gas smell, and each of those can turn a bad afternoon into something far worse. The safest moves for a homeowner are the simple ones: leave for gas, then shut off water and energy, then call for help.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and the right parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution rather than guessing, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Equally important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a modest repair bill versus the cost of a whole new unit. Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here's where to begin:

  • Consider an automatic leak shutoff that stops the cold supply the moment water hits the floor
  • Ask about a drain pan and drain line, especially for heaters in attics, closets, or above finished rooms
  • A 120F setting is safer for skin and easier on the heater than the hottest setting
  • Tag the water heater supply valve and the main shutoff so nobody has to search during a leak
  • Test the T&P valve annually; a valve that sticks or weeps afterward should be replaced
  • Annual flushing keeps sediment from causing the rumbling and hot spots that lead to leaks
  • Leave clear space around a gas heater and never store flammable liquids nearby
